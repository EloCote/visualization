# Data Visualization -- Visualization 1

    Link to dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/deaths-of-people-experiencing-homelessness/

    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

    I used Excel.

    > Who is your intended audience? 

    My intended audience is individuals working closely with people experiencing homelessness such as healthcare and social workers, as well as people working in law enforcement. 
    
    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

    With my visualization, I show the months of the year where there is the highest number of deaths of people experiencing homelessness, as well as the most common cause of deaths. This vizualisation helps sensibilize and inform people working with this population, so they can be prepared to assist people experiencing homelessness in the most critical months of the year (January and November). 
    
    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

    I focused on making the figures clear and easy to interpret. That is the reason why I chose a simple design (bar plots) to reduce the cognitive load.
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

    Unfortunately, with excel, it is hard to make the code reproducible as there is not a code that is easily sharable. This lack of reproducibility could make the public, and my intended audience, doubt whether I am presenting the data with integrity. 
    
    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  

    I ensured the data visualization was accessible by making sure the text was bigger than 12 points and by using a sans-serif font, Arial (so that it is more accessible with people with cognitive disabilities). I made sure that the colours of the different bars had enough contrast (to make them easier to tell apart) and used colours that were accessible for people with colourblindness (https://mk.bcgsc.ca/colorblind/palettes.mhtml).
    
    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  

    People experiencing homelessness and the people who are working with this population.
    
    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I thought it was important to show the months in which people experiencing homelessness are the most likely to past away, as this could directly inform interventions to help them. For example, organizations working with this population or the city of Toronto/Province of Ontario can mobilize more ressources during these months. The cause of death was also important as it helps guide interventions to assist this population. For example, the visualization shows that the most common cause of death is "Acute Drug Toxicity". 
    
    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?

    The "underwater labour" is all the work that was done to collect that data coming from "from TSSS, community partners, the Toronto Homeless Memorial, and the Office of the Chief Coroner (OCC) for Ontario".
