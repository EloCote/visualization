# Data Visualization -- Visualization 2

    Link to dataset: https://open.toronto.ca/dataset/hate-crimes-open-data/

    > What software did you use to create your data visualization?

    I used Python.

    > Who is your intended audience? 

    The intended is the general public and policy makers.

    > What information or message are you trying to convey with your visualization? 

    I want to sensibilize the general public about the prevalence of hate crimes in Toronto. To do so I show the prevalence of hate crimes per year from 2018 to 2025.

    > What aspects of design did you consider when making your visualization? How did you apply them? With what elements of your plots? 

    I wanted to show the trend over time of hate crimes rates. To do so, I did a simple line plot using the Seaborn library.

    > How did you ensure that your data visualizations are reproducible? If the tool you used to make your data visualization is not reproducible, how will this impact your data visualization? 

    I ensured the data visualization was reproducible by writing my code on python and adding comments to make sure people who want to reproduce my visualization know what each part of my script do.

    > How did you ensure that your data visualization is accessible?  

    I ensured my data visualization was accessible by not using overly bright colours (which can be overwhelming for people with autism or sensory issues), and by using a sans-serif font (easier for people with cognitive disabilities).

    > Who are the individuals and communities who might be impacted by your visualization?  

    Policy makers and communities who are affected by hate crimes in Toronto.

    > How did you choose which features of your chosen dataset to include or exclude from your visualization? 

    I wanted my visualization to be easily interpretable and convey one clear message so I only focused on frequencies of hate crimes per year.
    
    > What ‘underwater labour’ contributed to your final data visualization product?
    
    The "underwater labour" is all the  work that was done by the Hate Crime Unit employees to collect that data.
